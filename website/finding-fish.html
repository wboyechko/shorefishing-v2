Finding Fish

Learn how to put everything together and identify the places where fish are most likely to be holding from shore.

Fish Need Two Things

When shore fishing in a lake, river or stream, always remember that fish like two things: food and shelter.

Start looking for places where those two things come together. That simple idea can help you decide where to make your first cast.

Don't Just Pick A Spot

Look at the water and ask yourself: Where would food collect, and where could a fish stay protected?

If you see an area where the water slows down, pay attention to it.

Fish The Current

When fishing moving water, the original guide recommends casting upstream and allowing the current to bring your lure past the location you want to fish.

Think about where the current is moving and where a fish might be able to hold while food moves past.

06

Don't Always Cast Farther

One of the original guide's most useful observations is that casting as far from shore as possible doesn't necessarily mean catching more fish.

Smaller bait fish may stay closer to shore, away from currents, near structure or around a drop-off.

That means the water immediately in front of you can sometimes be worth more attention than the water far away.

07

Try Different Angles

Don't limit yourself to casting straight out from shore.

The original guide suggests trying a diagonal cast when conditions allow. This can keep your hook in a potential strike zone longer.

Change Your Angle

If one direction isn't producing, change the angle of your cast and work another part of the shoreline.

Experiment

This is one of the biggest lessons from the original ShoreFishing.net guide.

Try different locations, different lures and different times of the day or night.

Put It All Together

Start with food and shelter. Then look for current, depth, structure, bottom changes and shoreline shape.

Once you find something interesting, make a thoughtful cast and pay attention to what happens.

If it doesn't work, change something.

Keep Exploring

Don't limit yourself to one location or one technique. Finding fish from shore is a process of observation and experimentation.
