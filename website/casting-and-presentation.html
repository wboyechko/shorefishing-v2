Learn How To Fish

Casting & Presentation

Learn the basic casting motion, improve your accuracy and distance, and understand why putting your lure in the right place matters more than simply casting as far as possible.

Casting Is A Skill

Good casting comes with practice. The original ShoreFishing.net guide recommends learning the basic overhead cast first and developing accuracy before worrying about maximum distance.

The Main Difference

Spin-casting and spinning use a similar basic casting motion. The main difference is how the line is released.

01

Spin-Casting

The original guide describes spin-casting as one of the easiest styles to master. The reel sits on top of the rod and the line is released with the thumb.

  1. Press the release button with your thumb.
  2. Make sure the lure hangs a few inches below the rod tip.
  3. Face the direction you want to cast.
  4. Keep your casting elbow at your side.
  5. Bring the rod tip over your shoulder.
  6. Stop at approximately the 2:00 position.
  7. Make a quick, smooth motion forward.
  8. Release your thumb around the 11:00 position.
  9. Follow through until the rod is around the 9:00 position.

Think of the casting motion like a clock.

02

Spinning

With a spinning reel, the reel sits below the rod. The original guide says the line is controlled with the index finger.

  1. Flip the bail.
  2. Hook the line with your index finger.
  3. Let the lure hang a few inches below the rod tip.
  4. Face the direction you want to cast.
  5. Keep your casting elbow at your side.
  6. Bring the rod over your shoulder.
  7. Stop around the 2:00 position.
  8. Move forward smoothly.
  9. Release your index finger around the 11:00 position.
  10. Follow through to approximately the 9:00 position.

03

Practice Makes A Difference

The original guide recommends practicing in a field with a weight or bolt tied to the line.

01

Accuracy

Practice helps you learn when to release the line and place your lure where you want it.

02

Distance

Practice can also help increase casting distance.

03

Wrist Action

The original guide emphasizes a smooth forward motion and wrist snap.

04

Longer Isn't Always Better

At popular fishing spots, many anglers try to cast as far from shore as possible.

The original ShoreFishing.net guide points out that this doesn't necessarily mean more fish.

Smaller bait fish may stay closer to shore, away from currents, near structure or around drop-offs.

05

Cast To The Water You Want To Fish

When conditions allow and the shoreline isn't crowded, the original guide suggests casting diagonally rather than always casting straight out.

This can keep the hook in a potential strike zone longer.

Think About The Target

Don't just ask, "How far can I cast?" Ask, "Where do I want my lure to spend its time?"

The Goal

Accuracy comes before distance. Learn the basic motion, practice your release, and pay attention to where your lure actually spends time in the water.

As your casting improves, you'll be able to explore more of the shoreline and present your lure where you believe fish are holding.
