Reading The Shoreline

Learn how to read the water, shoreline and structure to find places where fish are more likely to hold.

Why Shorelines Matter

When you're fishing from shore, you can't move around the water the way a boat can. That makes learning to read the shoreline especially important.

Look for places where fish can find food, shelter, deeper water or a change in the movement of the water.

01

Current & Water Movement

Moving water can concentrate food and create places where fish can hold while waiting for something to come to them.

Watch the surface carefully. Look for areas where the current changes speed or direction.

What To Look For

A seam, current break or slower area beside faster-moving water can be worth investigating.

02

Breaks In The Water

A break is an area where the character of the water changes. It may be caused by current, depth, structure or shoreline shape.

These changes are worth exploring because they can provide both food and shelter.

05

Look For Structure

Structure provides another reason to stop and investigate a section of shoreline.

🌿

Weed Edges

Look along the edges of vegetation.

🌉

Docks & Bridges

Man-made structure can provide cover.

🪵

Logs & Trees

Fallen trees and logs can create cover.

🪨

Rocks

Rock piles and rocky areas can be worth exploring.

06

Bottom Transitions

Pay attention to changes in the bottom. A transition from rocks or sand to mud, or from mud back to sand or rocks, can be worth investigating.

Put It All Together

Don't look at just one feature. A location becomes especially interesting when several things come together.

Look for food, shelter, current, depth, structure and changes in the shoreline.

Experiment

Shore fishing is about learning the water. Try different locations, presentations and times, and pay attention to what changes when you get a bite.
